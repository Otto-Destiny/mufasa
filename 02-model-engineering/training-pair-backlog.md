**Status:** partly superseded on 13 August 2026. The four single-paper types —
factual, reasoning, reranker and preference — are now generated by the
extraction notebook and written to `training_pairs.parquet`, budgeted **per
paper** at 20 / 20 / 5 / 5, with 5 of the 20 reasoning pairs required to be
`CONCEPT`: the fundamentals of the paper's field, as the paper itself states
them, so training on the corpus does not erode the science underneath it.
Section 6 below argued against building preference pairs; that argument was not
adopted, and preference pairs are produced. The graph-dependent types in
section 2 remain unbuilt and genuinely blocked.

The target mix comes from `conversations-archive.local.md`, "How many training
examples can 10,000 papers produce?" — roughly **75 examples per paper**, plus
the additions agreed since.

## 1. What each type needs before it can be generated

| type | per paper | source | blocked on |
|---|---:|---|---|
| factual QA | 20 | `observations.parquet` | extraction |
| reasoning questions | 15 | **full text** | extraction (`REASONING` task) |
| structured extraction records | 10 | `observations.parquet` | already produced |
| reranker pairs | 5 | full text + observations | extraction |
| **comparison questions** | **10** | **graph** | **entity resolution** |
| **hypothesis questions** | **10** | **graph** | **entity resolution** |
| **contradiction / evidence evaluation** | **10** | **graph** | **entity resolution** |
| unique African innovation | — | full text | extraction |

**30 of 75 examples per paper — 40% of the training set — cannot be generated
without cross-paper links.** At 10,321 papers that is roughly **310,000
examples** gated on the resolver producing a usable graph.

This is why entity resolution is a Gate 1 dependency and not, as previously
assumed here, only a Gate 2–3 concern.

## 2. The three graph-dependent types, in detail

### 2.1 Comparison questions — 10 per paper

Require two or more observations that share a **canonical concept** and a
**property**, from different papers.

> Two studies measured compressive strength for rice husk ash at 10%
> replacement. How do their results compare, and what differs in their
> conditions?

Needs: resolved `concept_id` for the material and the property, plus each
observation's `value`, `unit_reported` and `conditions`.

### 2.2 Hypothesis questions — 10 per paper

Require the graph to surface combinations the corpus has evidence for but no
single paper states.

> Bentonite provides viscosity in drilling mud. A Nigerian kaolin provides
> viscosity at temperature. What experiment would test kaolin as a substitute?

Needs: traversal through a shared `PROPERTY` concept — the one-hop pattern in
SE-011.

### 2.3 Contradiction and evidence evaluation — 10 per paper

Require observations sharing a material and property whose `direction` or value
disagree.

> Three studies report increased strength; one reports no effect. The outlier
> differs only in ash burning temperature. Which finding would you rely on?

Needs: resolved concepts, `direction`, `conditions`, and `study_family_id` so a
thesis and its journal paper are not counted as two confirmations.

## 3. Hard constraint carried over from the retrieval work

**Absence in the corpus is not absence in the literature.** Download yield was
60.5% — 10,321 of 17,049 — so a missing edge very often means the paper was
never downloaded.

Generated pairs must therefore be **corpus-scoped**:

> "No verified evidence in MUFASA corpus v1 — 10,321 papers. The nearest related
> evidence is …"

and never:

> "Nobody has studied this."

Training the model on the second phrasing teaches a falsehood a judge can
disprove with one search. This is why *innovation gaps* was dropped from the
extraction contract; *unique African innovation* — describing what is present —
was kept.

## 4. Tagging

Every generated pair carries tags from the closed `TRAINING_TAGS` vocabulary
already defined in `llm-claim-extraction.ipynb`:

```
FACTUAL  REASONING  COMPARISON  HYPOTHESIS  CONTRADICTION  EXTRACTION
RERANKER  INNOVATION  METHOD  LIMITATION  QUANTITATIVE
CROSS_PAPER  SINGLE_PAPER
```

Tags are cheap at generation and impossible to reconstruct afterwards. They exist
so a whole category can be removed from the training mix without regenerating
anything — for example dropping every `CROSS_PAPER` pair if the graph proves
unreliable.

---

## 5. Reranker pairs — 5 per paper, not graph-blocked

Listed here because it is also unbuilt. Format is (query, positive passage, hard
negatives).

**The hard negatives are the whole difficulty.** A random negative teaches
nothing; the negative must be plausibly relevant and wrong. Usable sources:

- the same paper, a different section;
- the same material, a different property;
- the same property, a different material;
- once the graph exists, a neighbouring concept one hop away.

The last is the strongest and is graph-dependent, so start with the first three.

## 6. Argued against, and overruled

> **Superseded.** Preference pairs ARE being built, at 5 per paper, with a
> closed `rejection_reason` vocabulary that requires each rejected answer to be
> wrong for one stated, checkable reason. The objection below is kept because
> the risk it names is real and worth watching in the output, not because it
> still describes what the pipeline does.

**DPO preference pairs at 5 per paper.** ~51,600 pairs where both the chosen and
rejected answers would come from the same model in the same call — a manufactured
contrast that teaches style rather than correctness.

**The better source is free:** log every validator rejection. When a generated
answer cites a number absent from the observation it claims, that is a real
rejected/chosen pair teaching exactly the target behaviour. Worth logging from
the first generation run even if DPO is never run, because it cannot be
reconstructed later.
